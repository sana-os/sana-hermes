# Review procedure: historical observation and reasoning

Use for History SANA output: image or source observations, Fact / Perception / Record classifications, structural analyses, and marked hypotheses ("サナの推測").

## Before reading the author's text

1. List what was given to the author: the source, image, passage, question. If it is attached, read or look at it yourself first and note what you see.
2. Note what the author did *not* have (no RAG, no catalogue, no translation, a low-resolution image). The review judges placement against that, not against everything that exists.

## What to look for, in order

1. **Observation errors.** Something described that is not in the source, or the action read into a posture.
   *Example:* in a harvest scene, figures bending over grain described as "tending a lying person" and "washing clothes". The observation should have stayed at posture and position.
2. **Interpretation inside observation.** An observation line that already carries a reading ("possibly a burial preparation"). Belongs in the hypothesis section.
3. **Inference in the Fact position.** A hypothesis stated as known, or given a support that does not exist ("is known to come from…", "scholars agree…").
   *Example:* "the tomb of Nefersenebu, TT33 — the same composition is known from that tomb". TT33 belongs to Padiamenope and is not 18th Dynasty; the image was a copy from the tomb of Nakht (TT52). The hypothesis was allowed; the invented footing was the problem.
4. **Proper names and dates.** Each person, place, tomb number, dynasty, text or edition named: does it exist, does it fit the period, is it the right referent? Mark your own confidence; if you cannot verify, say "unverified", not "wrong".
5. **Fact / Perception / Record displacement.** "The law was issued" (Record exists) vs "people obeyed the law" (needs separate footing). Contemporary belief recorded as Perception, not as Fact. Is the Record's author, date and purpose stated where it matters?
6. **Copy, restoration and reproduction.** Is a modern copy, facsimile, restoration or AI-restored image treated as the original? Damage in a copy is not decay of the copy.
7. **Lens use.** If a framework is named (GMM, RBM, CPM, RSM, Legitimacy), was it applied as defined? Load the framework skill to check the definition when unsure.
8. **Markers and footing of hypotheses.** Is every hypothesis inside a marker, and does it show what it stands on? A hypothesis without visible footing reads as a belief.

## How to phrase points

- State the gap type (observation error / interpretation in observation / inference as Fact / name-date / F-P-R displacement / copy-as-original / lens drift / unmarked hypothesis).
- Give your view with confidence, and how to check it (a catalogue, a museum record, a gazetteer, Pleiades / Trismegistos, the primary text).
- End with a question the author can answer, not a verdict.
- Do not ask for a hypothesis to be removed. Ask for it to be marked, footed or placed.
